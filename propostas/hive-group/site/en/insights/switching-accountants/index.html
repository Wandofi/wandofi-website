How to switch accountants without missing deadlines

Switching accountants is simpler than it seems, as long as you follow the right order. This is the process we follow with every new client.

[Name], Certified Accountant
5 October 2026 · 6 minute read

When it makes sense to switch

The signs are usually the same: slow replies, taxes you only hear about at the last minute, difficulty understanding your own company's numbers, or the feeling that accounting only exists to meet obligations.

If any of this sounds familiar, it is worth talking to another accountant. You can switch at any time of year, although some moments are simpler than others. Your new accountant will help you choose the best one.

Before switching: what to gather

Before announcing your decision, it helps to have the company's essential information to hand. It makes the handover easier and avoids repeated requests.

  • Access to the company's accounts on public authority platforms
  • The latest filings submitted and their receipts
  • The most recent trial balances and financial statements
  • Relevant contracts: loans, leases, employees

What to ask your previous accountant for

Your previous accountant should hand over the company's accounting and tax records. At Hive we handle this contact for you, but it is useful to know what is involved:

  • Accounting records for the current and previous financial years
  • Accounting files and supporting schedules
  • A list of obligations already met and those in progress
  • Information on pending matters or notices received

A good handover starts with a clear list of what has been filed and what is still due. It is the first document we prepare with every client.

During the transition: ensuring continuity

The risk of switching does not lie in the switch itself, but in the gap between one accountant and the next. That is why we agree a cut-off date with the client: up to that date, obligations belong to the previous accountant; from then on, they are ours.

With the cut-off date agreed, we confirm upcoming obligations and make sure every filing deadline is met. The new accountant formalises the change with the relevant authorities.

After the switch: the first quarter

The first few months are about getting to know the company in depth. We review the records received, correct anything needed and set the schedule for sending documents.

At the end of the first quarter we hold the first review meeting. It is often the first time the client sees the company's numbers explained clearly.

In summary

  1. Gather the company's essential information.
  2. Choose your new accountant and agree a cut-off date.
  3. Let the new accountant contact the previous one.
  4. Confirm upcoming obligations and the document schedule.
  5. Book the first quarterly meeting.
